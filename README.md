# Memewitz Trust Field

A GTM and community-design repository for building a trust field around Memewitz's meme-coin experiment.

The first question is not “when do we pay for the DEX?”

It is:

> What can Memewitz, Garcia, and Leo make together that lets people become identifiable first believers without reducing them to exit liquidity?

The initial answer is an NFT community: a Print.World collection representing the first believers, wrapped around the token as a cultural and evidentiary layer. The collection should be transferable if a market exists, but must not be sold as guaranteed liquidity, guaranteed token value, or a guaranteed exit.

## The three-person coordination model

### Memewitz — the living experiment

Memewitz brings:

- the token and creator context;
- the bot-mediated market experiment;
- the public record of launches, losses, migrations, locks, burns, and uncertainty;
- the emotional material that makes the meme more than a ticker.

Memewitz should not be asked to manufacture certainty. The account's strongest asset is candid observation under pressure.

### Garcia — the collection and creator bridge

Garcia brings:

- creator-facing coordination;
- collection and Binder/Print.World context;
- the early-believer / “Aeons” framing;
- a practical route from a creator's work into a community object.

Garcia should help define what the NFT means and how a creator can participate without turning the collection into a promise about future price.

### Leo — the evidence and GTM layer

Leo brings:

- the trust-field architecture;
- the 101-item pre-DEX book and checklist;
- search, claim ledgers, receipts, and falsifiers;
- the Marvin distribution/evidence layer;
- coordination between the token experiment, the collection, and the public narrative.

Leo should keep the project honest about what is observed, inferred, unknown, and falsifiable.

## The emotional thesis

The meme is not just “buy this.” Its emotional field is:

- **Defiant** — refusing the ritual that says paying for the DEX is proof of worth.
- **Suspicious** — looking at bots, signals, wallets, locks, and migration mechanics without pretending the chart is a moral document.
- **Tender** — making room for people who want to belong before they want to trade.
- **Embarrassed but alive** — admitting drains, failed experiments, awkward funding, and unfinished infrastructure.
- **Playful** — using a fox, market, artifacts, and absurd evidence objects to make serious checks memorable.
- **Protective** — trying to prevent the next person from losing a deposit to a story they could not verify.
- **Patient** — making the DEX a consequence of community formation, not its substitute.
- **Collective** — asking the community to create identity, receipts, and meaning together.

The visual collection captures these emotions through a recurring orange-and-cream market clerk fox moving through a midnight market of evidence tables, bridges, locks, glass cases, blank receipts, maps, and shared mosaics.

## First community object: the believer collection

The collection should be designed around this sentence:

> These are the people who were here while the outcome was still uncertain.

Before minting, Memewitz, Garcia, and Leo must agree on:

1. collection name and visual identity;
2. supply and eligibility;
3. whether eligibility requires a token purchase, a contribution, a mission, or several paths;
4. whether and how an NFT is bound to the creator's token;
5. transferability and metadata permanence;
6. what the NFT means if the token migrates, stalls, or dies;
7. creator allocation and treasury policy;
8. private-data boundaries;
9. what is explicitly not promised;
10. the first shared community mission after minting.

No mint should be framed as a guaranteed financial opportunity. The collection's first job is to give the community a durable identity and a shared artifact.

## Safety boundary

This repository is research and community-design infrastructure. It does not:

- custody funds or private keys;
- ask for seed phrases or signatures through unverified links;
- execute trades;
- promise price appreciation, liquidity, migration, or NFT resale;
- publish raw private archives, credentials, or donor identities;
- treat token purchases, impressions, or volume as proof of trust.

Public minting, fundraising, token wrapping, or external account posts require separate explicit approval.
