# Values and emotions

The initial community cannot be built from mechanics alone. A DEX, token, bot, and collection can all exist while nobody feels that anything belongs to them.

The meme needs a legible emotional contract.

## Core values

### 1. Evidence over ceremony

Paying for the DEX is a ceremony. The trust field begins with receipts, questions, and shared work.

### 2. Belief before trading

A person should be able to identify as an early believer without being forced to become a trader.

### 3. Candor over confidence

Losses, awkward funding, bot mistakes, stalled migration, and uncertainty belong in the public record.

### 4. Transferable identity, not trapped capital

The NFT should be transferable if a market exists. It should not trap someone inside a story or imply a guaranteed exit.

### 5. Play as memory technology

The fox, market, glass cases, bridges, locks, maps, blank receipts, and shared mosaics make abstract trust questions emotionally memorable.

### 6. No anonymous community theater

Participation is opt-in. Private wallets and identities stay private by default. A visible holder is not automatically a public character.

### 7. Correction is contribution

A person who finds a mistake has increased the value of the field. Corrections should be collectible social acts, not punishments.

### 8. Patience as defiance

The community refuses to let urgency manufacture trust. “Not yet” is a valid state.

## Emotional grammar

| Emotion | What it feels like | What it produces |
|---|---|---|
| Defiance | “The DEX payment is not going to do our belonging for us.” | A non-payment first milestone |
| Suspicion | “Show me the mechanism, not the mood.” | Claim checks and falsifiers |
| Tenderness | “People should be able to join without becoming exit liquidity.” | Believer identity separate from exposure |
| Embarrassment | “We have failed in public and did not erase the evidence.” | Failure cards and corrections |
| Playfulness | “A fox with a magnifying glass can teach market structure.” | Memorable visual artifacts |
| Protection | “The next person should not pay for our uncertainty blindly.” | Preflight checklist |
| Patience | “The DEX can wait until the field exists.” | Seven-day experiments and gates |
| Collective pride | “We made this object and this evidence path together.” | Shared collection and missions |

## What the meme is not

- not a promise that the token is safe;
- not a claim that a bot creates organic demand;
- not a guarantee that an NFT will be liquid;
- not a replacement for on-chain verification;
- not a demand that the community fund infrastructure immediately;
- not a personality cult around a creator;
- not a performance of certainty.

## Visual rules

The 101-piece collection uses:

- a recurring orange-and-cream market clerk fox;
- a red scarf as the small mark of belonging;
- midnight blue for uncertainty and the unknown;
- amber for attention and human warmth;
- brass for mechanisms and accountability;
- glass for claims that can be inspected;
- electric cyan for signals that may be useful or misleading;
- blank paper for receipts that still need to be filled;
- bridges for migration and threshold decisions;
- mosaics for community-created meaning.

The images contain no intentional item text. Titles and numbers belong in metadata, captions, and the book—not inside the generated art.

## The emotional test

Before shipping a new meme, ask:

1. Does it make a mechanism easier to remember?
2. Does it preserve uncertainty rather than flattening it?
3. Does it invite a person to contribute something other than money?
4. Could it still mean something if the token failed?
5. Does it make the community look more like participants than an audience?
