# Community charter

## Purpose

Memewitz, Garcia, and Leo are coordinating a public trust field for a meme-coin experiment. The field gives early believers a shared identity, a public evidence standard, and a way to participate before the project asks them to pay for the DEX.

## Roles

### Memewitz

- supplies the live creator and bot context;
- publishes operating observations and failures;
- makes the project-specific claims that require receipts;
- retains responsibility for creator-side decisions.

### Garcia

- helps coordinate the creator and collection layer;
- advises on Print.World/Binder collection mechanics;
- helps turn the collection into a community object rather than a payment receipt;
- flags when a creator-facing promise is unclear.

### Leo

- maintains the GTM structure and search layer;
- translates observations into claims, artifacts, and falsifiers;
- maintains the public-safe repository projection;
- keeps the distinction between evidence, interpretation, and unknowns visible.

## Shared commitments

1. We do not call a purchase proof of trust.
2. We do not call an NFT liquid unless a real market demonstrates that it is.
3. We do not expose private wallet identities by default.
4. We do not ask for seed phrases, private keys, or unverified signatures.
5. We record mistakes with the same care as wins.
6. We distinguish token exposure from community membership.
7. We publish what a claim supports and what it does not support.
8. We define the falsifier before celebrating the signal.
9. We let the DEX remain optional until the field has produced evidence of belonging.
10. We make room for people to leave quietly.

## Decision protocol

For a new public claim or community mechanic:

1. Name the observation.
2. Name the proposed interpretation.
3. Name the unknowns.
4. Name the falsifier.
5. Assign an owner.
6. Choose a public artifact.
7. Set a review date.

No one collaborator can turn a hypothesis into a community promise by enthusiasm alone.

## First-believer collection rules

The initial Print.World collection must have a written constitution before minting:

- supply;
- eligibility paths;
- creator allocation;
- transferability;
- metadata permanence;
- token-binding semantics;
- treasury and fee policy;
- failure behavior if the token migrates, stalls, or dies;
- privacy policy;
- explicit non-promises.

A holder can transfer or sell an NFT later if a market exists. That is a property to design for, not liquidity to guarantee.

## Conflict and correction

If collaborators disagree:

- preserve both positions in the working ledger;
- identify the smallest experiment that separates them;
- do not publish the disagreement as resolved until the evidence exists;
- record the correction and who proposed it.

The community is not a cheering section. It is the sensor network.
